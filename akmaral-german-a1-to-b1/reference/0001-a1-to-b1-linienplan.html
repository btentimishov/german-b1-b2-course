Keep this route nearby

From first phrases to joining the conversation.

The route to B1 is a series of useful things Akmaral can do—not a giant grammar checklist. Each station adds independence: first communicate basic needs, then connect everyday experiences, then participate with reasons, plans, and opinions.

A1

Ankommen · Arrive

Introduce yourself, understand familiar words and very short messages, ask and answer simple personal questions, order basic things, and use numbers, days, and times with support.

A2

Unterwegs · Get around

Handle predictable everyday exchanges, understand short clear messages, describe home and routine, make simple arrangements, and talk briefly about past events, present needs, and future plans.

B1

Mitreden · Join in

Deal with most familiar situations independently, follow the main points of clear standard language, tell a connected story, explain reasons and plans, and write straightforward connected personal text.

A simple learning rhythm

01Enter one meaningful everyday scene.
02Notice one phrase or grammar pattern.
03Retrieve it without seeing the model.
04Use it in a true message or spoken turn.
05Return after 1, 3, 7, 14, and 30 days.
The B1 rule of thumb
Don’t wait for perfect grammar. Aim to keep the interaction moving: say the main point, add a reason or detail, ask a follow-up question, and use a repair phrase when something is unclear.

Level descriptions are practical paraphrases of the Council of Europe’s CEFR can-do descriptors. For reliable external practice across listening, reading, writing, and speaking, use the Goethe-Institut’s free practice materials and Deutsche Welle’s Nicos Weg A1–B1 workbook.
